₹ Value of Your Life A citizen-maintained public record · 2006 — present

After a preventable tragedy in India, the state settles on a price for your life. The going rate is about ₹6,00,000. What it almost never delivers is a conviction.

Every major mass-casualty disaster of the last twenty years — what happened, what was paid, who was blamed, and who, if anyone, actually answered for it. Compensation is counted here because it is usually the only accountability that ever arrives.

Submit an incident

Know of a tragedy that belongs on this record? Share a news link. The link is researched automatically — corroborating coverage is gathered from other outlets — and a maintainer verifies the facts before it appears here.

Submissions become public GitHub issues. Casualty and accountability details are verified editorially — automated research drafts the entry, a human publishes it.

About this record

Value of Your Life is a seed dataset compiled from public reporting — Wikipedia, news archives and court records. Death tolls use widely reported figures; where the official count is disputed, the entry says so. “Convicted” means convicted by a court — not arrested, not suspended, not transferred. Arrests and suspensions are listed separately because they are so often where the story ends.

The “value” shown per incident is the ex-gratia the state announced per deceased — the number a government reaches within hours of a tragedy it will spend decades not prosecuting.

This is a starting point, not a complete record. New incidents are gathered by a scheduled scraper (see scraper/) and reviewed by hand before entering the ledger. If an entry is wrong or out of date, correct it — that is the point of a ledger.
